# The setup interview

Ten minutes, nine questions. Run it once. Write the answers to `my-setup.md` in the skill folder,
then read that file before every build forever after.

If `my-setup.md` already exists, do not re-run this. Offer to edit instead.

## How to run it

Ask the questions one at a time. Wait for each answer. Do not batch them into a wall of text.
Push back once on a thin answer to question 1, then move on. After question 9, write the file,
show it back, and ask for one edit pass.

---

## Question 1: What do you actually watch, listen to, and know cold?

**This is the one that carries the whole system.** Pop culture is the memory hardware, not decoration.

A list of favorites is useless. What is needed is what the thing is about **structurally** — the
shape of it, so the shape can carry an idea.

Answer twice: the thing, and the job it can do.

> "Survivor" is useless.
> "Survivor is about making alliances you will have to betray, and everyone knows it in advance"
> carries game theory, incentive design, and the prisoner's dilemma.

> "Taylor Swift" is a favorite.
> "Taylor's Version is how you restate something correctly after getting it wrong the first time"
> is a tool you will reach for a dozen times a semester.

Ask for eight to twelve. Include work history, hobbies, and anything with a procedure to it.
Procedures are the best hooks of all, because they already have an order.

## Question 2: Who are your two podcast hosts?

One precise, one curious. Names, and three sample lines each so the voice is reproducible.

The curious one is the whole trick. Being confidently wrong out loud and then recovering is the exact
shape of a cold call going sideways, and hearing that happen is different from reading about it.

## Question 3: What are you taking, and what is the exam format?

Course codes, professor names, session cadence, and for each: laptop or paper, open or closed book,
notes sheet allowed or not, cold call culture, participation weight. This changes everything
downstream.

## Question 4: Where are you, physically?

Real places seen every day are free memory hooks. Buildings, the walk to class, the parking
structure, the coffee place, the route. A method-of-loci palace built from streets already walked
costs nothing to maintain.

## Question 5: How do you want to be talked to?

Tone, sentence length, formality, humor level. Also the negative: what kind of writing makes the tab
get closed. Be specific about banned constructions.

## Question 6: What is your career lens?

A future product manager reads a stats chapter differently than a banker. Name the target role, the
industry, and the two or three questions always asked of any new concept.

## Question 7: What is off limits?

Topics, jokes, framings, references. Taken literally and permanently. Written verbatim into
`my-setup.md`. Never argued with.

## Question 8: What does your AI policy say?

Read the course AI policy out loud before building anything. Paste the exact language per course.
Prep from assigned readings is usually fine. Anything touching graded work usually is not. The
syllabus is the only thing that counts, and if it is unclear the move is to ask the professor.

## Question 9: What are you actually worried about?

The real fear, named. Getting called on and freezing. Being the least quantitative person in the
room. Sounding like a tourist in a technical conversation. Every guide gets built partly to solve it.

---

## Writing the file

Write `my-setup.md` with one section per question, verbatim answers preserved. Add a
`Last updated:` date at the top. Then say this:

> Edit this file after your first real guide. That is when you find out what you actually wanted,
> which is never quite what you said in the interview.
