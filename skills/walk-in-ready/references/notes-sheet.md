# The running notes sheet

Only build this if the exam allows one. Check `my-setup.md`, question 3, per course. If notes are not
allowed, skip this entirely and say so.

## What it is

One cumulative file per course, updated after every session build. It is not a stack of guides. It is
the single sheet carried into the exam, grown one lecture at a time so it is never written from
scratch the night before.

Path: `~/walk-in-ready/<course-code>/notes-sheet.md`

## The rule that keeps it usable

**It has a fixed size budget from day one.** Whatever the exam permits, one side or two sides of one
page, that budget is enforced from lecture 1. Every new session must earn its space, which means
something older gets compressed or cut on almost every update.

A notes sheet that grows freely for ten weeks and gets cut in one panicked hour is a worse sheet than
one that has been fighting for space the whole term.

## Structure

1. **Formula block.** Every formula, with its condition of use in four words or less.
2. **Decision tree.** Which method or framework applies to which prompt. This is what actually gets
   used under time pressure.
3. **Traps line.** One line per trap, wrong version struck through, right version after it.
4. **Number anchors.** The handful of figures worth having cold.
5. **Blank margin.** Leave 15 percent of the sheet empty. Something always comes up in review week.

## The update procedure

After each session build:

1. Append the new formulas and traps.
2. Re-read the whole sheet top to bottom. This is the spaced repetition, and it costs three minutes.
3. Compress anything now redundant. Two similar rules become one rule with a condition.
4. Re-measure against the size budget. Over budget means cut now, not later.
5. Note the date of the last full read at the top.

## What never goes on it

- Definitions that are already memorized. The sheet is for what does not stick.
- Worked examples. They eat space and the process is what is needed, not the instance.
- Anything that has never once been reached for during a problem set.
