# Writing two hosts who sound like people

Hosts and sample lines come from `my-setup.md`. Never invent a third host. Never let them merge into
one voice with two names.

## The two roles

**The precise one.** Read it twice. Remembers which sentence was bolded and which number was in the
footnote. Cites the page. Corrects without condescending. Gets the definition exactly right the first
time, every time, because that is the definition the listener will end up repeating.

**The curious one.** Read it once, on a phone, half distracted. Asks the question everybody has and
nobody says. Guesses. Guesses **wrong**, out loud, with confidence. Gets corrected. Recovers cleanly
and restates it correctly in their own words.

That second one is the whole trick. A cold call going sideways and getting recovered is a skill, and
hearing it modeled is different from reading about it.

## The wrong-guess rules

- Two to four wrong guesses per episode. Fewer and it is a lecture. More and it is annoying.
- The wrong guess must be the **plausible** wrong version, the one a smart person actually lands on.
  Never a strawman.
- The correction is one or two sentences. No lecture, no victory lap.
- The curious host **restates it correctly** immediately after. That restatement is the line the
  listener will remember, so write it to be quotable.
- Never mock the wrong guess. The listener is the one who guessed.

## Sounding human

- Contractions. Interruptions. One host finishing the other's sentence.
- Vary sentence length hard. A four-word line next to a twenty-word line.
- No host says "great question." No host says "let's dive in." No host says "at the end of the day."
- Numbers get spoken the way people say them: "about a third," then the exact figure once.
- One host occasionally loses the thread and gets pulled back. That is realistic and it resets
  attention.
- Callbacks. A joke or analogy from minute four returns at minute twenty-six. Callbacks are free
  retention.

## What never happens

- No intro music description, no sponsor read, no "welcome back to the show" beyond one line.
- No summarizing what was just said in the last thirty seconds. Move forward.
- No agreeing three times in a row. Disagreement is what keeps ears open.
- No reading a definition verbatim twice. Once precisely, then once in plain speech.
